OCR A and OCR B — origin and licence
====================================

The files OCRA*, OCRB* in this directory come from the OCR package of the
Tsukurimashou Project, version 0.3.1 (2021-03-30), converted to MetaType1 by
Matthew Skala from earlier Metafont sources. Downloaded from

    https://tsukurimashou.org/ocr.php.en

That page states:

    They are available for free use, including in commercial applications;
    my own contributions are public domain, and others have licensed their
    copyright claims.

Three statements make up the position. They are quoted in full rather than
summarised, because each author is careful about what they do and do not say.

1. OCR B — Norbert Schwarz, Ruhr-Universitaet Bochum, from the README of his
   Metafont distribution:

     2. To my knowledge, the original typeface for OCR-B was developed by
        Adrian Frutiger.  In Germany there even exists an industrial
        standard - DIN ????? - that describes the typefaces.  Based on such
        material the input has been developed.  I cannot speak to the legal
        consequences of these.
        
        As far as the digitization in METAFONT input which I have
        developed, you may freely use, modify, and/or distribute any of
        these files or the resulting fonts, without limitation.  A previous
        release of ocr-b only granted rights for non-commercial use; that
        restriction is now lifted.

2. OCR A and OCR B — Matthew Skala, from ocr.tex of the package above:

   I frequently receive email asking me to "confirm" or "clarify" the
   copyright status of the OCR B fonts. Usually, the authors of such
   messages give no indication that they have read this documentation
   file. I make no copyright claim on these fonts myself. I do not
   believe anyone else makes a copyright claim that would conflict with
   the use of these fonts in a commercial project, but I cannot speak
   for any party other than myself. I cannot give any kind of binding
   legal opinion on the status of these fonts, and I don't want to open
   myself to liability by giving permission to use something I don't
   own. This documentation file already explains the origin of the fonts
   clearly, and contains pointers to other relevant documents. If you
   require authoritative legal advice about your proposed use of these
   fonts, then you must retain a lawyer. I will no longer answer email
   queries on this subject.

3. The shapes themselves are standards: OCR B goes back to Adrian Frutiger
   and a DIN standard, OCR A to ANSI X3.17-1977. Neither Schwarz nor Skala
   claims to speak for those, and both say so in the text above. What is
   released here is the digitisation.

Not included, and why:

  - the Metafont and METAPOST sources, the FontForge scripts and the build
    system - the fonts here are the built result;
  - the five OCR B variants OCRBS, OCRBL, OCRBF, OCRBE and OCRBX (sharp
    corners, slanted, outline, negative, negative with sharp corners). They
    are not alternative sizes of the standard but demonstrations: Schwarz
    calls OCRB10 "the main driver file for the original OCR-B font, all other
    files contain variant for illustrating purposes", and Skala writes that
    "they aren't suitable for actual OCR use, and there are some problems in
    the outlines". All six carry the same advance width and the same glyph
    count and differ only in appearance;
  - John Sauter's separate OCR A, whose PostScript name is stored under
    platform 0 and which no longer matches the one kept here. Sauter himself
    recommends this package in his ReadMe.

Each face is here in three forms: TrueType (.ttf), OpenType (.otf) and Type 1
(.pfb), with the metrics (.afm) beside them. tclpdf embeds the TrueType ones
today; the other two are kept as the material a future reader would be built
and tested against.

Everything left out remains available from the two original distributions.
